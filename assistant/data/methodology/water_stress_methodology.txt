WRI AQUEDUCT 4.0 - WATER STRESS METHODOLOGY

Definition
Water stress measures the ratio of total freshwater withdrawal to total available renewable freshwater resources in a country or basin. WRI Aqueduct 4.0 provides country-level water risk scores on a scale of 0 (low stress) to 5 (extremely high stress). The score used in this dashboard is the 2030 Business-as-Usual projection using Total Gross Withdrawal weighting.

Data sources
Aqueduct 4.0 was developed by the World Resources Institute using hydrological modeling. Input data includes gridded precipitation, evapotranspiration, and runoff from the PCRGLOBWB 2 global hydrological model. Water demand data comes from sectoral consumption statistics from the FAO, AQUASTAT, IEA, and national water agencies. Future projections use CMIP6 climate scenarios and SSP (Shared Socioeconomic Pathway) population and economic growth models.

How the risk score is calculated
The raw score from Aqueduct ranges from 0 to 5 (baseline) or can be negative in supply-abundant regions. For this dashboard, 2030 Business-as-Usual scenario scores with Total Gross Withdrawal weighting are used as the closest proxy to near-term risk. Raw scores are normalized to a 0-10 scale using min-max normalization across all countries. A score of 10 indicates the highest projected water stress; 0 indicates the lowest.

Water stress categories (Aqueduct original scale):
  0.0 - 1.0: Low stress (<10% withdrawal-to-availability ratio)
  1.0 - 2.0: Low-to-medium stress (10-20%)
  2.0 - 3.0: Medium-high stress (20-40%)
  3.0 - 4.0: High stress (40-80%)
  4.0 - 5.0: Extremely high stress (>80%)

Interpretation in ESG context
Water stress affects industries that rely on freshwater in their operations or supply chains, including agriculture, food and beverage, semiconductors, textiles, chemicals, and energy production. Companies sourcing from high water stress regions face operational disruption risk (suppliers unable to operate due to water scarcity), regulatory risk (water use restrictions), and reputational risk. Water stress is increasingly linked to social conflict and migration, compounding other ESG risks. Countries with risk scores above 7 in this dashboard include parts of the Middle East, North Africa, and South Asia.

Limitations
Country-level scores mask significant within-country variation; river basin and local data provide more precise assessments. The 2030 projection carries model uncertainty, particularly for precipitation scenarios. The metric measures withdrawal-to-availability ratios and does not directly measure water quality, groundwater depletion, or seasonal variability.
